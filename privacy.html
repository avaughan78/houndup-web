Privacy Policy

Effective 16 September 2026 · Operated by Andy Vaughan (sole trader)

Hound-Up is a business management app for solo dog walkers — scheduling, client and dog records, and invoicing. It's built to work entirely offline. This page explains, plainly, what data the app handles and where it goes.

Your data, and automatic iCloud backup

Everything you enter — client details, dog details, schedules, walk records, notes, and invoices — is stored locally on your device using Apple's on-device database (SwiftData), and backs up automatically to your own private iCloud account via Apple's CloudKit, so it carries over to your other devices too. There's nothing to turn on for this — it works the same way iCloud backs up your Photos, as long as iCloud itself is switched on for apps on your iPhone (the default). This sync is between your device and Apple directly — Andy Vaughan has no server that stores or has access to this data, and cannot see, access, or receive it at any point. Apple's own privacy policy governs how iCloud handles it.

One deliberate exception: your own business profile (your name, business name, address, rates, bank details, logo) is not included in this automatic iCloud backup — it stays only on this device unless you also sign in with Apple (see below), which is what carries it to a new device or to a second device instead.

Signing in (free, optional)

Hound-Up also offers an optional, off-by-default way to sign in with Apple — from Settings, or the first time you send an invoice while signed out (invoicing itself requires it, since invoice numbers and Stripe payment links are issued by our backend, not generated purely on-device). This is free, and separate from Autopilot below. Signing in creates a real account and copies your business data to our own backend, hosted on Supabase, so it carries over to a new device or a second device — this is what backs up the one thing iCloud alone doesn't (your business profile).

If you sign in, the following is copied to our backend:

  • Your business details (name, address, rates, bank details) — the one part of your data that doesn't otherwise leave this device
  • Your clients' names, contact details, addresses, and their dogs' details, including medical/vet notes
  • Your schedules, walks, sits, and notes
  • Your invoices and payment records, including a copy of each sent invoice PDF
  • Your Stripe Restricted API key, if you've connected online payments — so payment links keep working even if your phone isn't reachable at the time
  • A device push token, if you turn on any push notification (for example, being told the moment a client pays)

This is never sold, used for advertising, or shared beyond what's already listed under Sub-processors below. Signing out (Settings → Autopilot → Sign out) stops any further syncing but doesn't delete what's already stored. To permanently and immediately delete everything stored about you, use Settings → Autopilot → "Delete cloud data" — this never affects your own device's data (or its iCloud backup), which stays exactly as it is.

Autopilot (optional, paid)

Autopilot is a separate, additional paid subscription (currently £9.99/month, billed through your Apple ID; see the Terms of Service for billing details) — it requires being signed in (above) but doesn't change what's synced or copy anything new. What it actually adds: certain features run on a schedule on our servers instead of only when you have the app open — automatic payment reminders, automatic visit-summary emails, your calendar feed, your availability calendar, and instant booking-request notifications. It isn't a backup feature — your data already backs up (via iCloud automatically, as long as that's switched on for the app, or to our backend once you're signed in) whether or not you ever turn Autopilot on. Apple, not Hound-Up, processes the subscription payment itself and holds your payment details — we only know that you're subscribed, not your card details.

Autopilot is entirely optional and off by default. If you never turn it on, the features listed above only run while you have the app open — everything about signing in, above, still applies exactly the same either way.

Public micro-site — Autopilot (optional)

Autopilot also includes an optional public micro-site (Settings → Profile → Micro-site), off by default — a shareable business page you can post on Instagram, Facebook, or WhatsApp. The business details, bio, badges, and other fields on it are already stored on our backend as part of Autopilot above; turning the micro-site on simply makes a subset of them — your business name, logo, bio, "Insured"/"DBS checked" badges, qualifications, specialities, service area, rates, and up to three stats — visible on a public page reachable by anyone with the link, or who finds it via search.

You can also feature specific dogs on the page — off by default for every dog — which shows that dog's name and existing photo (already stored for your own records, not newly uploaded for this). As with all client data (see "Your clients' data" below), it's your responsibility to have your client's agreement before publishing their dog's name and photo this way.

The page is served at houndup.atomlabs.dev/m/..., via a Cloudflare Worker that proxies our backend. The link itself is permanent once generated — switching the micro-site off stops the page from showing anything to visitors (they see a simple "not currently available" message) rather than changing the link, so a link you've already shared publicly keeps working if you turn the feature back on later. If you never turn this on, nothing here applies to you.

Sending an invoice by email

When you choose to email an invoice from inside the app, the following is sent, only for that purpose, to a backend that relays it on to your client:

  • The recipient's email address (as you've entered it for that client)
  • The invoice PDF — which includes your business name, address, contact details and bank payment details, and your client's name, address, and a summary of the walks being billed
  • The email subject and message text

This is relayed through two services, solely to deliver that one email:

Supabase — runs the backend that receives the request and forwards it to Resend. It also stores a device attestation key for your phone (see below), so the sending feature can't be abused by requests that didn't come from a genuine copy of the app. This key isn't linked to your name, your clients, or any account — there are no accounts.

Resend — the email delivery provider that actually sends the message to your client's inbox.

None of this is stored for any purpose beyond delivering that email, used for marketing, or sold or shared with anyone else. If you never use the "email invoice" feature, none of this ever happens — the separate "Share" option instead hands the PDF straight to iOS's own share sheet, reaching only whichever app you pick there (Mail, WhatsApp, AirDrop, etc.), and doesn't touch our backend at all.

Reading a booking message

The "read a message" feature (for turning a pasted booking request into a scheduled walk or sit) sends the message text you paste in to Microsoft Azure OpenAI, which returns the booking details it found — who, when, any notes — back to the app. Nothing is retained by us once the response comes back. This only happens when you actively use this feature.

Booking requests and enquiries from your clients

If you've turned on Booking Availability or the public micro-site, a visitor to your micro-site can submit a booking request or a general question directly to you. Whatever they enter — their name, a phone number or email if they give one, and either their requested date/time and a note (for a booking) or a free-text message (for a question) — is sent to our backend and stored only until your device next fetches it (typically within moments, whenever you have the app open or a push notification wakes it), at which point it's deleted from our backend entirely. If push notifications are on, the request's headline details are also delivered to your device the same way as any other push.

This is the one place Hound-Up's backend handles personal data belonging to someone who isn't a Hound-Up user themselves — your prospective client. It's used solely to deliver their message to you; it's never used for anything else, and isn't affected by whether you're signed in.

Publishing a calendar feed

If you turn on "Publish a calendar feed" in Settings, your booked walks and sits (dates, times, and dog names) are uploaded to a file on our backend (via Supabase), at a link only you have. Anyone who has that exact link can view it, which is why the in-app confirmation warns against sharing it publicly. Turning the feed off removes the file. This is entirely opt-in and off by default.

Contacting support

Messages sent via Settings → Contact support are relayed through the same backend and Resend to the developer's own inbox, along with a reply-to address if you gave one, purely so we can reply to you.

App Attest (device check)

Before any of the backend features above run, Apple's App Attest generates a one-time cryptographic key for your device and confirms with Apple that it's a genuine, untampered copy of Hound-Up. This is a standard anti-abuse mechanism — it identifies your device, not you, and carries no personal information.

Microphone & speech-to-text

If you use voice-to-text for walk notes, the audio is transcribed entirely on your device using Apple's on-device Speech framework. Nothing is recorded, stored, or sent anywhere.

What Hound-Up doesn't do

  • No account or sign-in required to use the app, or for your data to back up automatically via iCloud — Autopilot (Settings, off by default) is the one optional exception; see above
  • No advertising or ad tracking
  • No analytics or usage tracking
  • No selling of data, and no sharing with third parties beyond what a feature you actively chose to use needs to do its job

Sub-processors

These are every third party any backend feature above can send data to, and exactly what each one sees:

Supabase (EU, eu-west-1) — hosts the backend itself and the calendar feed files. For every walker, sees whatever a given request sends, as described above. Additionally, for any walker who's turned on Autopilot, stores the business/client/schedule/invoice data listed in that section, your device push token, and, if provided, your Stripe Restricted key — until you delete it or delete your account.

Cloudflare — if you've turned on the public micro-site, proxies that one page so it renders correctly as a webpage. Sees only what's already public on that page for visitors who load it.

Resend — delivers invoice and support emails. Sees the recipient address, subject, body, and any PDF attached to that one email.

Microsoft Azure OpenAI — parses pasted booking messages. Sees the message text you paste in, for that one request.

Stripe — only if you've connected online payments for your own clients to pay by card. This is your own Stripe account, connected directly by you; when a client pays an invoice online, Stripe processes that payment and the card details involved. Hound-Up never sees or stores your clients' card details itself, only whether an invoice has been marked paid.

Apple (iCloud/CloudKit) — automatic backup, under your own Apple ID. Sees everything except your own business profile fields (see "Your data, and automatic iCloud backup" above) — never shared with us.

Apple (App Store) — bills and manages the Autopilot subscription entirely through your Apple ID. Hound-Up never sees your payment details, only that you're subscribed.

Apple (Push Notifications) — if notifications are enabled, Apple's Push Notification service delivers them to your device using the device push token described above.

postcodes.io — Today's weather icon looks up your business postcode (from Business Details) to get its coordinates, sent directly from your device. Free, no API key, no account.

Open-Meteo — fetches the current weather for those coordinates, via our own backend rather than directly from your device — nothing else about your business is sent for this.

We won't add or replace a sub-processor for these features without updating this list first.

Your clients' data

Hound-Up is a tool you use to run your own dog-walking business. The personal details you enter about your clients (names, addresses, contact details, emergency contacts, and their dogs' medical/vet information) are your responsibility to collect and use appropriately — for example, telling clients you keep these records and only using them for the purposes they'd reasonably expect (scheduling and invoicing their walks).

Your control over your data

You can edit or delete any client, dog, or walk record at any time from within the app, or use Settings → Data → "Erase all data" to wipe everything on the device (and its iCloud backup) at once. Deleting the app from your device removes the local copy; a copy may still remain in your iCloud account until you clear it from your device's own iCloud settings, or until Apple's own retention policy removes it. If you've turned on Autopilot, use Settings → Autopilot → "Delete cloud data" to separately and permanently erase everything it stored on our backend — this is independent of your iCloud backup, so deleting one doesn't delete the other. If you've turned on the public micro-site, switching it off (Settings → Profile → Micro-site) immediately stops the page showing anything publicly.

Children

Hound-Up isn't directed at children and isn't intended for use by them.
